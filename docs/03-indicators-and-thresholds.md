# 03. Indicators and thresholds

> For: developers, crew members and people presenting the Orbit Health prototype.
> Sources: approved research D7, self-assessments D8, guidance D10, NASA-STD-3001, OCHMO briefs, NOAA SWPC and identified Earth guidelines. Each row includes the label and URL of the value's source.

## 1. How to read classes and colors

Each indicator has a main class. A class describes where the information comes from; it does not make a diagnosis.

| Class | How the software uses it |
| --- | --- |
| `requisito_oficial` | NASA or an official agency published a numeric requirement or target. Show the citation and the applicable mission profile. Do not turn a countermeasure target into an individual diagnosis. |
| `referencia_terrestre` | General clinical range marked [EARTH-GUIDELINE]. The screen must display "Earth reference, not a flight limit". A value outside the range calls for context, not a diagnosis. |
| `linha_de_base` | No reliable numeric flight limit. Compare the person with themselves as described in docs/04; deviation and trend reach yellow at most. |
| `gatilho_sintoma` | Red comes from alarm symptoms described in an approved source or procedure, not from a sum of symptoms. The class may coexist with additional triggers in another class. |

The levels in docs/05 use the "Health:" prefix. Level 0 is gray for missing, invalid or expired data. Level 1 is green for valid data inside the applicable range and without relevant deviation. Level 2 is yellow for review by the crew member. Level 3 is static orange for action by the CMO and flight surgeon (in the original `docs/07` it was red; ORBITA split the two colors, see `docs/18`, section 2.2). Level 4 is static red, reached only by an alarm symptom, with the instruction "Contact CMO now" for immediate risk. The levels are interface states, with no physical unit. Nothing blinks or emits sound; the prototype does not use the labels Caution, Warning or Emergency and does not replace the station alarm.

**Final color = highest level among threshold, personal deviation and cross-referencing.** The z-score alone never produces red. The deviation band is a prototype rule [INFERENCE, docs/04]: 2 to 3 standard deviations gives yellow; more than 3 standard deviations in 2 consecutive readings gives persistent yellow. The baseline needs 7 valid readings and is not updated with yellow or red days. A bad, uncalibrated or expired reading is gray. The body map shows the worst state of the region. Source: [docs/04, formula and rules](04-data-cross-referencing.md), [docs/05, levels and scenarios](05-software-architecture.md), [NASA-STD-3001, scope and tailoring](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/1-0-scope/).


### How the engine uses regraCor

The engine evaluates `regraCor.regras` in the declared order. A satisfied condition proposes its level; symptom and cross-referencing rules are evaluated separately, and the final state is the highest level. Without a valid, calibrated reading within its validity period, the state is level 0, gray. A valid reading with no alert rule stays at level 1, green. The baseline uses `|zScore|`: from 2 standard deviations, level 2; above 3 standard deviations in 2 consecutive readings, persistent yellow. The baseline alone never generates red. These cutoffs are a prototype rule [INFERENCE, docs/04]. Level 4 can only come from a symptom; no numeric value triggers it.

| Worked example | Evaluation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A, `ppco2_modulo`, peak of 2.95 mmHg in scenario C1 (sustained EWMA) | It is below the official maximum of 3 mmHg (V2 6004, 1 h average) and above the demonstration marker of 2.8 mmHg, which only applies inside rule R-CO2-01. | Level 2 (yellow) if it co-occurs with a symptom or a PVT worsening above 10% in the 3 h window; without co-occurrence, green with trend. A value of 3.2 mmHg would be level 3 (action by environmental control and contact with the CMO). Scenario C1 was adjusted by the coordinator on 15/11 to 2.95 mmHg. |
| B, `spo2`, reading of 92% | The Earth reference is 95% to 100%; 92% also reaches the Earth cutoff for clinical contact. This label remains an Earth reference, not a flight limit. | Level 3, clinical contact according to the Earth source, with no automatic diagnosis. |
| C, `hrv_rmssd`, personal deviation of 2 standard deviations | No published flight cutoff exists; the baseline rule reaches the yellow marker. | Level 2, review trend and context. The marker never leads to red on its own. |

## 3. Check-in self-assessments

The daily check-in proposed in D8 takes 2 min. The indicator for hours of sleep obtained, ID `sono_tst`, is stored in min per the canonical unit in docs/06. Previous sleep is recorded in min, together with perceived quality; sleepiness is a local question or the KSS when version, translation, purpose and license are approved. Mood is an in-house answer of 1 to 5 points, per screen T3 and the readiness index A12 in docs/04; energy remains an independent local control. Pain uses an NRS of 0 to 10 points if the instrument is approved. Symptoms are separate items, with no sum. Thirst uses a local scale of 0 to 10 points; intake is mL since the last record. Appetite and incomplete meal are independent answers. In ORBITA, the ID `apetite` uses an ordinal category of 1 to 3 (1 normal, 2 reduced, 3 greatly reduced), with no cutoff and compared only with the personal baseline; the 0 to 100 proposal of D8 remains as a future alternative, with no automatic conversion between the two. Each answer generates a manual Leitura (Reading), with UTC time, origin and quality.

The ID `humor` uses an in-house scale of 1 to 5, per T3 and A12. This choice resolves the divergence with the 0 to 100 point proposal recorded in D8; the D8 source must be aligned in a future review. Do not convert historical answers without an explicit rule. The ID `fadiga` remains tied to the scale stated in the authorized protocol; do not infer equivalence with the KSS.

The weekly check-in of D8 takes 10 min and may include instruction plus a 3 min PVT-B, if the medical team approves and the device is validated. The RST flow studied on the ISS is approximately 5 min, including a brief survey and a 3 min PVT-B; the current operational calendar is not public. In the PVT-B, 355 ms is the lapse definition in brief-protocol literature, and 500 ms is the standard comparison definition; neither is a clinical alert threshold. The engine compares the personal series. NASA-TLX Raw records 6 task dimensions without diagnosis; use the authorized form and attribution, without aggregating incompatible scales.

Amsler is an assisted monocular test, with the same distance, correction and lighting; an image or distortion does not diagnose SANS. Near acuity uses a recorded chart and protocol. The urine color scale is a self-reported record, not an automatic camera inference. POMS, PHQ-9, GAD-7 and other closed instruments do not enter the check-in without a license, clinical governance, validated translation when needed and a human response. The script duration and instruments follow [D8, implementable self-assessments](../pesquisa/bruto/08-autoavaliacoes-implementaveis.md) and the conditions of use recorded there.

## 6. What NASA does not publish and how the software handles gaps

Research D7 found no public NASA flight cutoffs for heart rate, blood pressure, SpO2, body temperature, blood glucose or blood count. For these signs the table shows only a labeled Earth reference, baseline, context and symptoms. There is also no published universal threshold for HRV, body mass, fatigue, mood, sleep, PVT lapses, visual acuity, Amsler, intraocular pressure, urinary calcium, cytokines, urine color, appetite or blinks per minute from EOG in flight.

NASA publishes countermeasure targets for bone, strength and aerobic capacity, as well as environmental and radiation protection requirements. This does not authorize inferring an individual diagnostic cutoff. Requirements vary with tailoring, vehicle, instrument, task and program. The career value in mSv is not interchangeable with organ dose in mGy-Eq or mGy. Temperature and humidity must be evaluated by the combined region of the requirement, not by independent ranges. Noise must be evaluated by the per-band curve and dose, not by an isolated global approximation.

When there is no public numeric range, the software shows a trend with a baseline and does not color by a fixed threshold. Personal deviation can raise only to yellow. An urgent symptom uses an approved procedure and human contact. Without valid data or after the technical validity period, the screen is gray. No alert changes environmental equipment, chooses a shelter, prescribes medication or replaces the official station alarm and procedure.

## 7. Fields used beyond docs/06

The configuration preserves `id`, name, scope, body region, unit, direction, ranges, baseline, validity, source, explanation and guidance from docs/06. To meet the approved classification, `classe`, `valorDeDemonstracao`, `demonstracao`, `demonstracao.aplicaSomenteEmRegra` and `gatilhosSintoma` were added. `faixas.valoresPublicados` separates values from the source; the demonstration cutoffs stay outside that block. The `orientacoes` block includes `colega` and `gatilhoParaSubir`, in addition to the action lists. The configuration adds the declarative array `regrasCruzamento`, with conditions, window, level, hypothesis, guidance, sources and escalation fields. Proposal for a future update of docs/06: type these fields, formally separate published range from demonstration, and specify the shape of the rules and triggers. docs/06 was not changed.

The master table documents 74 indicators: the 73 from D7 and `eog_piscadas`. The JSON file contains 37 IDs: the 35 exact ones from the profiles draft and the two from ORBITA, `apetite` and `eog_piscadas`, which still need to enter the simulation profile. The remaining indicators without a profile stay as an approved reference in D7 and are not in the executable array until they are included in the profile.
