## 1. Short answer

- **Designed for Mars.** The farther from Earth, the more medicine needs to work onboard. On Mars, each message takes 4 to 24 minutes per leg and solar conjunction can cut direct contact for about 13 days to three weeks. Orbit Health was designed for this: offline, with an explained alert and "no data" separated from "normal".
- **Next steps.** There is no single door to the ISS. The path is bench, pilot on the ground and analog, an eligible partner and call, and only then NASA ethics, software and cargo safety. It is a multi-year plan (estimate of 3 to 6 years or more), and the Brazilian team has no automatic eligibility.
- **On Earth too.** The same logic serves where distance, confinement or unstable connection hinder care (Antarctica, Amazon, Indigenous territories, platforms), as a complement to teams and telehealth, never as diagnosis.

### 2.2 The reshaped Artemis, the Orion and the Gateway

- **Artemis III** became a low Earth orbit demonstration, planned for 2027, with 14 days planned, to test the Orion docked with commercial lander demonstration vehicles. **Artemis IV** is the lunar landing mission, planned for 2028. NASA has not yet designated the first crewed lander, and the pages consulted do not say whether Artemis IV will go through the Gateway. [OFFICIAL, [NASA, acceleration plan](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-shares-plans-to-accelerate-artemis-return-to-moon/); [NASA, Artemis III](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasas-artemis-iii-mission-to-test-future-moon-lander-operations/); [NASA, landers for 2028](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-partners-accelerate-lander-development-for-2028-moon-missions/)]
- **Gateway** is a small lunar station, intermittently occupied, operated remotely when empty. The current pages foresee the initial elements no earlier than 2027, up to four people for 30 to 90 days with the Orion and about 125 m³ habitable and 63 tonnes with the complete station (values for the whole station, not for the medical area). The Crew Health and Performance subsystem covers environmental health, medicine, countermeasures and behavioral health, and a technical concept describes a data layer that joins radiation, biosensors, exercise, medications, exams and records, shown to the crew member and sent to the ground. It is architecture under development (the concept itself calls itself a draft), not an operating lunar clinic. The PPE (power and propulsion element) has 60 kW, propulsion power and not a medical software budget. [OFFICIAL, [Gateway, capabilities](https://www.nasa.gov/gateway-capabilities/); [NTRS, Crew Health and Performance](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220014610/downloads/Crew%20Health%20and%20Performance%20101%20-%20September%202022.pdf); [NTRS, medical system concept](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200001341/downloads/20200001341.pdf?attachment=true)]

**EIMO** (Earth Independent Medical Operations) is the gradual transfer of part of the care and decision from the ground to onboard resources. The ground team remains in planning, prevention and non-urgent follow-up; in an emergency, the crew needs to act without an instant response. The research foresees a system that joins personal records, sensors, environment, behavior, laboratory, exercise, medications and inventory, with guided protocols and trend tracking for the Crew Medical Officer (CMO). ### 2.4 Partial gravity and dust

Gravity is about 1/6 g on the Moon and 0.38 g (about 3/8 g) on Mars. The transition between microgravity, partial gravity and Earth can affect orientation, balance, coordination, locomotion, motion sickness and orthostatic tolerance (standing without a pressure drop). There is no direct human evidence of long exposure to lunar or Martian gravity; the estimate that less than 0.4 g may not preserve muscle, bone and conditioning is extrapolation, not a clinical threshold. The NASA-STD-3001 requirements (aerobic capacity of at least 36.5 mL/(min·kg) for surface EVA; strength and aerobic capacity at least 80 % of baseline) are task fitness, not diagnostic cutoffs. [OFFICIAL, [NASA, body in space](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/the-human-body-in-space/); [NTRS, partial gravity](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210019591); [NTRS, post-flight assessment](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250009058/downloads/McPhee%20Doc.pdf); [OCHMO, exercise](https://www.nasa.gov/ochmo-tb-031-exercise-overview-2/)]

**Dust.** NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 2 Rev. F sets, for particles smaller than 10 µm, a time-weighted average below 0.3 mg/m³ for lunar dust (intermittent daily exposure up to six months) and 0.1 mg/m³ for Martian dust (up to 30 days), with a recommended instantaneous peak of up to 10 mg/m³; the standard acknowledges limited data on authentic Martian dust. [OFFICIAL, [NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 2](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/nasa-std-3001-vol-2.pdf); [NASA, Martian dust](https://www.nasa.gov/general/establishing-crew-exposure-limits-of-martian-dust/)] These values are **not** in [`03-indicators-and-thresholds.md`](03-indicators-and-thresholds.md), where `exposicao_poeira` has no fixed cutoff. ### 2.6 Paragraph ready for the "Designed for Mars" slide

> **Designed for Mars.** On Mars, each message takes 4 to 24 minutes to reach Earth, and solar conjunction can cut direct contact for about 13 days to three weeks. NASA calls EIMO the gradual passage of the medical decision onboard. Orbit Health was designed for this scenario: it works without a network, compares each crew member with their own baseline, explains each alert with the readings, the rule and the source, and never confuses "no data" with "normal". In the operational vision, it stores everything onboard and, when the link returns, sends the urgent summary first; the emergency decision stays with the crew, and the flight surgeon receives the evidence when able to respond. Moon, Gateway and Mars change the priorities (radiation, dust, partial gravity, duration), not the rule: a trend or missing data never becomes a diagnosis.

Sources to put in the slide footer: [Goddard](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/goddard/space-communications-7-things-you-need-to-know/), [NTRS, communication with Mars](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240011265/downloads/12-3-24%2020240011265.pdf?attachment=true) and [NTRS, EIMO](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240001402). The "was designed for" part describes the prototype; "in the operational vision" describes what does not yet exist. [INFERENCE, track 16, D5]

### 3.3 Ethics, safety and software: what funding does not replace

**Ethics.** Research with human subjects involving astronauts or taking place on the ISS goes through the NASA IRB (institutional review board) and the HRMRB (Human Research Multilateral Review Board), with the ISS partners, which meets every quarter. Participation is voluntary: the consent briefing usually takes place about a year before launch, and each astronaut chooses which studies to take part in and can withdraw without penalty. Public cap of crew minutes per study and average time between ethics approval and flight: **NOT FOUND**; the IRB does not reserve a launch slot. [OFFICIAL, [IRB Submission Guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/irb-submission-guidance/); [NASA IRB and HRMRB](https://www.nasa.gov/irb-hrmrb/); [consent in orbit](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230002830/downloads/HumanResearchOnOrbitConsentProcess_POIWG_April2023.pdf)]

**Hardware safety.** The review follows four phases (0, initial technical meeting; I, preliminary design; II, critical design; III, final acceptance), and passing a phase is not flight authorization. [OFFICIAL, [Payload Design Guide](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/iss-payload-design-guide-20240515.pdf)] The standards are in [`09-orbit-glasses.md`](09-orbit-glasses.md), section 10; track 17 adds:

- Battery (JSC-20793 Rev. D, active) and materials (NASA-STD-6001B Change 3, active since Jun. 9, 2025): a commercial LiPo cell or USB power does not demonstrate compliance on its own. [OFFICIAL, [JSC-20793](https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/JSC/JSC-20793_D); [NASA-STD-6001](https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/NASA/NASA-STD-6001)]
- Touch temperature: the limits depend on the material and contact time (high of 43 °C or 49 °C, low of 0 °C or 15 °C), so 43 °C is not a universal limit. Noise: NC-40 in sleep areas on missions above 30 days, personal audio in the ear below 115 dBA (ceiling, not recommended volume) and intermittent noise during sleep up to 10 dBA above background. [OFFICIAL, [NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 2](https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/NASA/NASA-STD-3001_VOL_2); [acoustics](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/nasa-std-3001-vol-2.pdf)]
- Bluetooth and Wi-Fi are not prohibited, but another device connected on the station does not approve Orbit; the recommended first configuration is local recording with the radio off. Universal wearable certification deadline: **NOT FOUND**. [OFFICIAL, [Bluetooth on the ISS](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/stationreport/2021/08/25/iss-daily-summary-report-8-25-2021/); INFERENCE, track 17, D3]

**Onboard software.** The NPR 7150.2D standard (in force until Mar. 8, 2027) classifies software by use, criticality and human dependence; flown research software is usually Class C, but Orbit should not assume this, because a system that influences a medical decision may require another class. [OFFICIAL, [NPR 7150.2D](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7150_002D_&page_name=AppendixD); [NASA ROI, requirements](https://tsc.jsc.nasa.gov/roi-swprocess/sw_reqs.html)] HRP guidance asks for 64-bit Windows 10, no communication dependency outside the NASA domain and data in files, and advises against an iPad app. [OFFICIAL, [HRP](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/for-prospective-researchers/)] After selection for flight, the SSC (Station Support Computer) flow takes about 1.5 to 2 months between initial submission and final delivery, excluding development, security and uplink. [OFFICIAL, [SSC Tablet Flow](https://tsc.jsc.nasa.gov/roi-swprocess/ssc-tablet_flow.html)] Precedent: the ISS Food Intake Tracker (ISS FIT) was sent in June 2016 and installed by Kate Rubins on the station iPads on Aug. 11, 2016; much of the effort was integrating it into the ISS server. [OFFICIAL, [NASA, ISS FIT](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/hhp/nutritional-biochemistry/)] The offline prototype without remote updates ([`../AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md), rule 4) already goes in the requested direction.

### 3.5 The pilot: 8 to 12 participants, focus on feasibility

**Proposed design, not approved by NASA** [INFERENCE, track 17, D2]: prospective, within-subject, crossover pilot study, with randomized and balanced order, equal to that of [`09-orbit-glasses.md`](09-orbit-glasses.md), section 9.2. Each participant is their own control: A, routine without glasses; B, sensors on and audio off; C, audio chosen by the crew member only in pre-sleep. A condition D (comparison audio) only enters if the hypothesis asks and the participants accept.

- **Size:** 8 to 12 astronauts accumulated over several expeditions, with five evaluable nights per condition. It is a planning goal, not a statistical power calculation nor a selection commitment. Reference: the 2024 Canadian call considered up to 14 participants in five years with less than five hours of crew time highly feasible, and of low feasibility above 20 participants or 15 hours. [OFFICIAL, [CSA AO 2024](https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/funding-programs/funding-opportunities/ao/2024-health-life-sciences-research-on-the-iss.asp)]
- **What it can conclude:** feasibility, adherence, comfort, data quality, variance estimate and preliminary signal. It does not prove efficacy.
- **One primary outcome, chosen beforehand:** sleep duration by actigraphy or self-reported sleep quality. Secondary: sleep diary, PVT (Psychomotor Vigilance Task, five-minute task in the NASA PVT+ guide), KSS (Karolinska Sleepiness Scale, from 1 to 9), mood, comfort and preference. [OFFICIAL, [NASA PVT+](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-02-27-nasa-pvtuser-guide.pdf); [Standard Measures](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230014025/downloads/SSM%20IWS24%20Spaceflight%20Standard%20Measures.pdf)]
- **Audio during sleep stays out of the first pilot.** EEG and EOG enter as exploratory; sleep staging is not diagnosis.
- Record what affects sleep (light, caffeine, medication, EVA, noise, workload) and how much time each interaction consumes, including setup and cleanup.

**Adjustment in the presentation script (applied on 11/15).** The 6:40 slide in [`08-prototype-plan-20h.md`](08-prototype-plan-20h.md), section 6, was renamed "crossover pilot with the glasses and path to the ISS", because tracks 4 and 17 recommend that each person be their own control, more realistic than an independent control group.

### 4.3 Older adults, chronic disease and shift work

- **Home monitoring.** A 2024 systematic review describes remote monitoring as a complement to services and highlights the human component; a scoping review with adults aged 65 or older found 19 studies, with little participation of older adults in the design. [PAPER, [npj Digital Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01182-w); [scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13002154/)] In a retrospective heart failure study (74 patients, median of 327 days), personalized heart rate thresholds had a higher positive predictive value than two simple comparators, but worsening episodes went undetected. [PAPER, [EHJ Digital Health](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjdh/ztad049)] With 76 people monitored by wearable, simulations generated from 65 to 3,113 alarms depending on the rule, the frequency and the smoothing; this is **not** a false alarm rate. [PAPER, [Zahradka et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9854741/)] Lesson for Orbit: a personal baseline helps, but does not eliminate false alarms nor replace the clinical flow.
- **Shifts and confinement.** NIOSH links fatigue to lower reaction time, attention, short-term memory and judgment, and NASA studies circadian misalignment and sleep loss in operations. [OFFICIAL, [CDC/NIOSH, 07/14/2026](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/fatigue/about/index.html); [NASA Fatigue Countermeasures Laboratory](https://www.nasa.gov/human-systems-integration-division/human-performance/fatigue-countermeasures-laboratory/)] In a review of 21 studies with embarked workers, 91.6 % cited at least one environmental factor that disturbed sleep, and noise was the most cited (62.4 %). [PAPER, [Kerkamm et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8750891/)]
- **Sleep sounds.** A 2026 review (15 studies, 2,024 hospitalized patients) found a favorable aggregate result with music, nature sounds and white noise, but with high heterogeneity and only in hospital. A study validating glasses, bone conduction, EEG or EOG and soundscape for shift workers: **NOT FOUND**. [PAPER, [Henriques et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13675385/)]

### 4.6 Paragraph ready for the "On Earth too" slide

> **On Earth too.** The same support logic can be useful where distance, confinement or unstable connection hinder the continuity of care. An offline app that records how each person usually is, organizes self-assessments and explains changes in simple language can complement local teams, telemedicine and home follow-up. In Brazil, this can inspire projects co-created with rural, riverside and Indigenous services, respecting privacy, language and autonomy. Orbit is a support prototype, not a diagnosis, a promise of clinical result or a substitute for professionals.

## 5. What not to say

| Phrase to avoid | Why the sources do not support it | Say this instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The glasses improve sleep" or "reduce fatigue" | No study validates the glasses combination; the sounds review is hospital-based and heterogeneous (section 4.3) | "We will measure whether it is feasible and comfortable; benefit only with a study" |
| "The pilot will prove it works" | 8 to 12 participants serve for feasibility and variance (section 3.5) | "Feasibility pilot, with each crew member as their own control" |
| "The Brazilian team can propose directly to NASA" or "the AEB takes us to the ISS" | Eligibility depends on each call; 1997 arrangement suspended; active agreements have another scope (section 3.2) | "We seek an eligible PI and institution, with AEB support" |
| "Winning the challenge takes us to the ISS" | NASA says a prize is not equivalent to a grant, an integration contract or flight access (section 3.1) | "The prototype is step zero of a path of years" |
| "In three years we will be on the ISS" | 3 to 6 years or more is an estimate and may grow (section 3.6) | "Multi-year plan, conditioned on funding and reviews" |
| "We will test at CHAPEA" or "at NEEMO" | CHAPEA 2 ended on Oct. 31, 2026 with no open window; NEEMO was discontinued in 2023 | "HERA by call or Concordia until Aug. 1, 2027" |
| "Artemis III lands on the Moon" | Artemis III is a low Earth orbit demonstration in 2027; the landing is on Artemis IV, in 2028 | "The next crewed lunar landing is planned for 2028" |
| "Artemis II showed that the crew's health..." | Results not published up to 11/15/2026 | "Artemis II collected sleep, cognition and samples; the analysis continues" |
| "The Orion monitored vital signs all the time" | Not documented: actigraphy, radiation, on-demand devices and exams by phase | "The Orion measured radiation continuously and sleep by actigraphy" |
| "On Mars the physician answers in 24 minutes" | 24 minutes is one leg; round trip takes 8 to 48 minutes before queue and evaluation, and there are blackouts of days | "The answer can take almost an hour, or weeks in conjunction" |
| "The Mars network will solve communication" | It relays, does not eliminate the delay; bandwidth goals are not a guarantee | "More bandwidth helps; the delay remains" |
| "Orbit already meets the Gateway requirements" | Gateway is architecture under development; medical budget not published; nothing was qualified | "Designed with the principles that NASA describes for EIMO" |
| "Below 0.4 g the body loses bone mass" | It is a hypothesis by extrapolation, with no direct human evidence | "There are no long-term human data in partial gravity" |
| "Technology approved by NASA" or "endorsed spinoff" | Spinoff is not an endorsement (section 4.1) | "Based on research and knowledge published by NASA" |
| "NASA already does this offline on Earth" | The Ejenta case uses the cloud | "NASA's precedent is connected; Orbit works offline" |
| "Oil platforms have no physician" | NR-37 requires a professional, infirmary and telemedicine according to size | "It can support the onboard professional between contacts" |
| "Ready for the SUS" or "regularized device" | No Anvisa classification and no adoption in the SUS | "Prototype; framing depends on the declared purpose" |
| "Offline solves privacy" | The LGPD still applies | "Offline reduces exposure; consent and governance remain" |
| "Half of the Amazon UBS have no internet today" | 2021 data, UBS outside capitals, and 49 % had internet | "In 2021, 49 % of UBS outside the capitals of the Legal Amazon had internet" |
